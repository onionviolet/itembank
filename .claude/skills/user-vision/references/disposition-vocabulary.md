# Disposition vocabulary

**Before using this: if the host project already keeps an idea ledger, adopt it.**
Check its vocabulary against the seven below and its rejection rows against the
required fields. Add what is missing to the ledger that exists. Do not open a
second one.

Coalescing a project into one coherent product means organizing viable breadth
into one architecture. It does not mean deleting ideas for minimalism. Every
substantial proposal receives exactly one disposition.

## The seven dispositions

- **Core:** required shared behavior or foundation. Other capabilities depend on
  it. Build it first.
- **Registered:** a composable capability, mode, or strategy that ships under the
  same authority, accessibility, permission, recovery, and maintenance contracts
  as Core, but is optional or selectable.
- **Prototype:** useful, but awaiting evidence or interaction validation before a
  durable commitment. Build it reversibly.
- **Backburner:** viable but not timely. Retain its dependency, its cost driver,
  and an explicit revisit trigger.
- **Deferred:** waiting for named evidence, authority, or external state that
  does not yet exist. Name what unblocks it.
- **Superseded:** its purpose survives through a better mechanism. Point to the
  mechanism that carries the purpose forward.
- **Rejected:** conflicts with a named product rule or quality requirement. Goes
  in the append-only rejection ledger with every field below.

## Required fields per active disposition

For Core, Registered, Prototype, Backburner, Deferred, and Superseded, record:

- The idea, stated concretely.
- Its disposition.
- Its owner.
- Its evidence class: fact, inference, or recommendation.
- Its dependency and cost driver.
- For Backburner and Deferred: the revisit trigger or unblocking condition.
- For Superseded: the mechanism that now carries its purpose.

## Rejection: the append-only ledger

A rejected idea is never silently removed. Each keeps:

- Original proposal and origin.
- Evidence considered.
- Exact reason, stated concretely, not as a vague preference.
- The conflicting rule: the specific product rule or quality requirement it
  breaks.
- Alternatives retained that serve the same underlying want.
- Date.
- Reconsideration condition: what would have to change to revisit it.

## Two rules that bind hardest

- **Simplicity alone is never a rejection reason.** If an idea is viable but does
  not fit now, it is Backburner or Deferred, not Rejected. Reserve Rejected for a
  concrete conflict with a named rule.
- **Rejections are append-only.** Removing a rejected idea erases the reasoning a
  future agent needs to avoid relitigating it. Keep the row.

## Why disposition beats reduction

A project that reduces its idea set for neatness loses the reasoning behind each
cut, and later agents rediscover and re-argue the same ideas. Disposition keeps
the full set legible while sequencing the work, so breadth survives without
forcing everything to ship at once.
