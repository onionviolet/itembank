<section id="the-airway-step-by-step"><h2>The Airway, Step By Step</h2><p>Airway management starts with a single question: can this patient move air on
their own? Every decision that follows -- positioning, adjuncts, suction,
ventilation -- hangs off that answer.</p>
<p>When the airway is at risk, the first intervention is positioning. A
head-tilt/chin-lift opens the airway in most patients with no suspected spine
injury; a jaw-thrust does the same without moving the cervical spine.</p>
<p>The positioning steps, in order:</p>
<ol><li>Open the airway with a head-tilt/chin-lift.</li><li>Suction the oropharynx if it is not clear.</li><li>Insert the airway adjunct that fits the patient.</li><li>Reassess air movement after every move.</li></ol>
<p>Signs to track while reassessing:</p>
<ul><li>Air movement at the mouth and nose</li><li>Chest rise with each breath</li><li>Noisy breathing that worsens with position</li></ul>

<section id="when-to-call-for-help"><h2>When to Call for Help</h2><p>Some findings mean the airway is already failing: snoring respirations with a
weak effort, cyanosis, or a patient who cannot speak in full sentences.</p>
<p>Calling for help is not a failure of the first responder. It is the second
intervention, and it happens while the first one is still being done.</p>
<p>Call early and call once, with the essentials: <strong>name</strong>, role, and the
airway finding. A <em>repeat</em> of the same information delays help. Use a
<code>run report</code> for the handover, and see <a href="call-checklist.md">the call checklist</a>
when the airway is already failing.</p>
