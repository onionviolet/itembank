## LESSON

### Scene Priorities

Context before the action: a responder's first job on every scene is to
decide whether the scene is safe to approach and what the single greatest
threat is. That decision comes before any patient contact.

The steps, in order:

1. Confirm the scene is safe.
2. Identify the number of patients.
3. Summon additional resources before committing.

Signs recorded on arrival:

- Chief complaint
- Mental status
- Airway status

| Sign | What to check | First action |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Snoring | Airway patency | Head-tilt/chin-lift |
| No response | Mental status | Stimulus check |

The learner's task is to predict the correct first action before reading
the key: an obstructed airway is opened by positioning, not by suction.

Q1. Which first action addresses a snoring airway?   (difficulty: application)
[LESSON-REF: Scene Priorities]
[OBJECTIVE: emt:scene.priority]

A) Suction the oropharynx
B) Head-tilt/chin-lift
C) Reposition the stretcher
D) Apply oxygen

CORRECT: B

WHY BEST: Head-tilt/chin-lift opens a tongue-obstructed airway directly;
suction only clears fluid, which is a different problem.

KEY DISCRIMINATOR: The first action must open the airway itself rather
than treat a finding elsewhere.

DISTRACTOR ANALYSIS:
- A) Suction clears fluid, not a tongue obstruction; it would be correct if the airway were blocked by secretions.
- B) Correct: the jaw and head position open the obstructed passage.
- C) Repositioning does not open the airway; it would be correct if the question asked about spinal precautions.
- D) Oxygen is delivered after the airway is open; it would be correct if the question asked about ventilation.

TRAP: Choosing any intervention that happens after the airway is open.

CONFIDENCE: high
