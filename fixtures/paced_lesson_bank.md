## LESSON

A glimmerkeeper tends glimmer moths, whose light output is the whole point
of the craft. Before any technique, hold the one rule: a moth dims before it
fails, so the keeper's job is noticing dimming early, not fixing failure
late.

[STEP: orientation]

### What A Keeper Watches

The keeper watches three signals: wing sheen, pulse rhythm, and rest posture.
Sheen fades first, rhythm stumbles second, and posture collapses last, so the
order of the signals is also the order of urgency.

A moth with dull sheen but steady rhythm is asking for food, not rescue.

[STEP: mechanism]

### Why Dimming Happens

Dimming is a fuel problem before it is anything else. The light organ burns
nectar sugars, and the burn rate rises with cold air, so a cold night both
demands more fuel and dims the light that would show the shortfall.

The keeper's counter-move is simple: feed before the cold, not after the dim.

[STEP: application]

### Reading A Dim Moth

Put the signals together. Dull sheen with steady rhythm means feed. Dull
sheen with a stumbling rhythm means warm the enclosure first, because a cold
moth cannot eat. Collapsed posture means both, warmth first, then food by
hand.

The mistake to avoid is treating every dim moth as a feeding case; the
stumbling rhythm is what separates hunger from cold.

- D) Sheen is a readout, not a surface to treat; this would be correct if the question asked which signal fades first.
