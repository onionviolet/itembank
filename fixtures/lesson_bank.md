## LESSON

### The Airway, Step By Step

Airway management starts with a single question: can this patient move air on
their own? Every decision that follows -- positioning, adjuncts, suction,
ventilation -- hangs off that answer.

When the airway is at risk, the first intervention is positioning. A
head-tilt/chin-lift opens the airway in most patients with no suspected spine
injury; a jaw-thrust does the same without moving the cervical spine.

The positioning steps, in order:

1. Open the airway with a head-tilt/chin-lift.
2. Suction the oropharynx if it is not clear.
3. Insert the airway adjunct that fits the patient.
4. Reassess air movement after every move.

Signs to track while reassessing:

- Air movement at the mouth and nose
- Chest rise with each breath
- Noisy breathing that worsens with position

| Sign | What it means | Action |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Snoring | Tongue against the pharynx | Head-tilt/chin-lift or jaw-thrust |
| Gurgling | Fluid in the airway | Suction |
| No air movement | Complete obstruction | Immediate intervention |

### When to Call for Help

Some findings mean the airway is already failing: snoring respirations with a
weak effort, cyanosis, or a patient who cannot speak in full sentences.

Calling for help is not a failure of the first responder. It is the second
intervention, and it happens while the first one is still being done.

Q1. Which finding most clearly suggests the airway is at risk?   (difficulty: application)
[LESSON-REF: The Airway, Step By Step]
[OBJECTIVE: emt:airway]

A) A patient speaking in full sentences
B) Snoring respirations with a weak effort
C) A capillary refill of two seconds
D) A pulse oximetry reading of 98 percent

CORRECT: B

WHY BEST: Snoring with a weak effort means the tongue is obstructing a
diminishing air movement, which is the definition of an at-risk airway.

KEY DISCRIMINATOR: The finding must show that air movement itself is
threatened, not that the patient looks unwell some other way.

SECOND-BEST: A. Full sentences mean the airway is patent right now, and this
would be correct if the question asked which finding clears the airway.

DISTRACTOR ANALYSIS:
- A) A patient speaking in full sentences has a patent airway; this would be correct if the question asked which finding rules out obstruction.
- B) Correct: snoring with a weak effort is obstruction with failing compensation.
- C) A two-second refill is a circulation finding, not an airway finding; this would be correct if the question asked about perfusion.
- D) A normal reading does not describe air movement; this would be correct if the question asked about oxygenation only.

TRAP: Reaching for any abnormal vital sign instead of the one finding that
describes air movement itself.

CONFIDENCE: high

Q2. Which two findings together point to an airway that is becoming obstructed?   (difficulty: application)
[LESSON-REF: The Airway, Step By Step]
[TYPE: multi]
[SELECT: 2]
[OBJECTIVE: emt:airway]

A) Noisy breathing that worsens when the patient relaxes
B) A patient who answers a question in one word per breath
C) A complaint of thirst
D) A heart rate of 140 in an adult
E) Skin that is warm and dry

CORRECT: A, B

WHY BEST: Both describe air movement degrading -- noise that changes with
position and speech compressed to one word per breath. Together they mean the
obstruction is progressing, not resolving.

KEY DISCRIMINATOR: The pair must both be about the airway, and both must show
a change over time rather than a single snapshot.

SECOND-BEST: Not applicable; the remaining options describe perfusion and
hydration findings with no airway content.

DISTRACTOR ANALYSIS:
- A) Correct: position-dependent noisy breathing is obstruction in progress.
- B) Correct: one word per breath is a failing effort to move air.
- C) Thirst is a dehydration finding; this would be correct if the question asked what suggests poor perfusion.
- D) Tachycardia is a compensation finding; this would be correct if the question asked how the body responds to stress.
- E) Warm dry skin is a normal perfusion finding; this would be correct if the question asked what rules out shock.

TRAP: Counting every abnormal finding as an airway finding, when only the
findings that describe air movement belong here.

CONFIDENCE: high

Q3. Explain why repositioning is the first intervention when an airway is at risk, and name the finding that would make you switch to a jaw-thrust.   (difficulty: analysis)
[TYPE: short]
[OBJECTIVE: emt:airway]

MODEL: Repositioning is first because most obstruction is the tongue against
the posterior pharynx, and a head-tilt/chin-lift moves the tongue forward with
the jaw; it is fast, reversible, and requires no equipment. The finding that
switches the technique to a jaw-thrust is a suspected cervical spine injury,
because the jaw-thrust opens the airway without moving the neck.

RUBRIC:
- Names repositioning as the first intervention because tongue obstruction is the usual cause
- States that a suspected cervical spine injury is the reason to use a jaw-thrust
- Notes that the jaw-thrust avoids moving the neck

TRAP: Treating equipment as the first step, or using a head-tilt/chin-lift
without considering the cervical spine.

CONFIDENCE: high
