## LESSON

### Managing the Unconscious Patient

An unconscious patient cannot protect their own airway, and the first job of
the responder is to protect it for them. Positioning comes before equipment:
a recovery position keeps the tongue from falling back and lets secretions
drain instead of pooling.

Every decision that follows -- suction, adjuncts, ventilation -- is a
response to what the airway is doing right now, not to what the patient was
doing before they lost consciousness. Reassessment happens continuously,
because the airway can fail again after it has been opened.

### Oxygen Delivery Basics

Oxygen is delivered, never stored. A non-rebreather mask with a high flow
delivers a high concentration while the reservoir bag stays inflated; a
nasal cannula delivers a lower concentration that is still enough for many
patients who are breathing adequately on their own.

The delivered concentration depends on the patient's breathing pattern, not
just the device. A mask that looks correct but sits loose delivers less than
the number on the dial suggests, which is why reassessment after application
is part of the skill.
