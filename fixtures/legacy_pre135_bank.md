# Fictional pre-13.5 shift-handover bank (synthetic legacy fixture)

Fully invented content for a fictional overnight facility handover procedure.
It exists as a legacy artifact for the Phase 16C upgrade audit: plain lesson
prose with no callouts, no glossary section, and no term references, which is
what a pre-13.5 lesson looked like. It is not derived from any real course,
exam, or textbook.

## LESSON

### What the handover covers

The invented handover is a spoken summary plus a written log. The spoken part
is for what changed during the shift and the written part is for what the
next operator must check first. Neither replaces the other, and the drill in
this fixture is about deciding which facts belong in which.

### The three facts that always transfer

Three facts move across every handover in this fictional procedure: what is
currently out of service, what was tried and did not work, and what the next
operator has been asked to watch. Anything else is optional context that the
log already carries.

### Where handovers go wrong

The invented failure mode is a handover that reports actions instead of
states. Saying that a valve was cycled twice tells the next operator what
happened; saying that the valve is now closed and holding tells them what is
true. The procedure asks for the second.

Q1. Which fact belongs in the spoken part of this fictional handover?   (difficulty: application)
[ID: 6c1b90ea44d24f10]
[OBJECTIVE: handover / spoken summary]

A) The full equipment inventory
B) What changed during the shift
C) The written log's page count
D) The next shift's staffing roster

CORRECT: B

WHY BEST: The spoken part of the invented procedure exists for change; the
written log already carries the standing detail.

KEY DISCRIMINATOR: Whether the fact is a change or a standing condition.

DISTRACTOR ANALYSIS:
- A) Inventory is standing detail; it would be correct if the question asked what the log carries.
- B) Correct: change is what the spoken summary is for.
- C) A page count is bookkeeping, not a handover fact.
- D) Staffing is scheduling; it would be correct if the question asked who to call.

TRAP: Treating anything written down as unnecessary to say aloud.

CONFIDENCE: high

Q2. Which handover sentence follows the procedure in this fixture?   (difficulty: application)
[ID: 91f7a2cd58b34e07]
[OBJECTIVE: handover / states not actions]

A) I cycled the intake valve twice
B) The intake valve is closed and holding
C) I spent most of the shift on the intake valve
D) The intake valve has been a problem lately

CORRECT: B

WHY BEST: The invented procedure asks for the state that is true now, not the
sequence of actions that produced it.

KEY DISCRIMINATOR: Whether the sentence names a state or an action.

DISTRACTOR ANALYSIS:
- A) An action, not a state; it would be correct if the log asked for a work record.
- B) Correct: a state the next operator can act on.
- C) Time spent is neither a state nor an actionable action.
- D) A general impression, which the procedure explicitly excludes.

TRAP: Reporting effort instead of condition.

CONFIDENCE: high
