## What going further could mean

The current product contract proposes a course built from the learner's books,
notes, syllabus or examination blueprint across subjects. A useful advantage
would combine appropriate readings, original visual explanations, practice,
projects and source-linked evidence within that personal course, with editable
files and recoverable local state. This is a product hypothesis, not evidence
that itembank already outperforms a named platform.

Parity has separate dimensions: available capabilities, authored curriculum
breadth/depth, coherent interaction quality, and learning transfer. A functioning
editor is not a full coding curriculum, and a large catalog is not evidence that
an individual learner can apply a concept. No aggregate parity percentage is
justified by this sampled comparison.

## Bounded next step and retained breadth

Recommended next step: review the existing native coding unit as one complete
learner journey, then choose one original visual unit in a second subject.
Inspect orientation, useful feedback, resumability and unaided transfer before
expanding course breadth. Reuse existing features when the gap is their joined
flow. Instructional authorship, substantial projects and maintained curricula
remain real ongoing work even when the platform primitives exist.

The five comparisons are benchmark routes, not new feature commitments or a
replacement capability ledger. P1/P2 are prototype comparison tasks under their
existing owners. P3/P4 retain their existing evidence/reviewer routes. P5 reuses
the settled retention integration. Wider LMS, exam-prep, note-taking, simulation
and language-learning references remain in the absorption plan with their
existing dispositions. No previously viable idea is rejected by this shortlist.
