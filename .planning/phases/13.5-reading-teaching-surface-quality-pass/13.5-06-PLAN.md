<objective>
Make the stem legible on first paint, and make the reader's section nav real.

ROADMAP criteria 1 and 4, which share one root cause and one fix. The sticky
context line is `position:sticky; top:0` and 1px-bordered; on first item render
the served client calls `card.scrollIntoView({block:"start"})`. At `scrollTop:0`
the card is already fully visible, but `block:"start"` scrolls its top edge to
viewport y=0 anyway, and the sticky band — one row at 768px and up, one or two
below — then covers the first line of the stem. The same missing property is why
a glossary `Full entry` link, a `Back to first use` link and every section-nav
anchor land under the header.

Criterion 4's other half: the reader section nav and the back-to-text links were
`display:none` on screen until 2026-08-12, because a print rule leaked out of its
`@media print` block. That leak is fixed; this plan is the first time the nav has
been designed against, and it is the revisit `03.1-UI-SPEC.md` §7.1 explicitly
deferred to the Phase 3.2 corpus, which has since landed.

Purpose: criterion 1 is the phase's headline failure and the cheapest to fix
correctly; criterion 4 is the orientation affordance a nine-section lesson needs
and a two-section lesson does not.

Output: one scroll contract shared by every surface, a band that cannot grow,
and a section nav that decides for itself whether to appear.
</objective>

  <name>Task 1: The scroll contract — one property, one guard, one measured height</name>
  <files>surfaces/presentation.py, surfaces/quiz_page.py, surfaces/lesson.py</files>
  <read_first>
    surfaces/presentation.py SHARED_CSS's `html` and `.context-line` rules and
    `context_line()`, which emits the stable `data-surface-context` hook.
    surfaces/quiz_page.py's two `card.scrollIntoView({block:"start", …})` call
    sites — one in `OFFLINE_JS`, one in `SERVED_JS` — and the `.context-line`
    rule in its style block. surfaces/lesson.py's heading, glossary and gate
    anchor targets.
  </read_first>
  <action>
**The scroll container is the document, and that was verified rather than
assumed.** Neither in-scope surface constrains height or sets `overflow` on
`body` or `.wrap`; the only `overflow:auto` regions are inner content wrappers
(`.case pre`, `.table-wrap`, `.scroll`, `.lesson-table-scroll`,
`.lesson-math-display`, the gloss panel) and none of them contains a scroll
target. **Binding rule to carry in a comment:** if a later shell puts the
activity canvas inside a nested overflow container, `scroll-padding-top` moves
to that container in the same change — a `scroll-padding-top` left on `html`
while a nested container scrolls is silently inert, which is this defect again
with a new cause.

(1) **In `presentation.py` SHARED_CSS**, so every surface inherits it:

  `html{ scroll-padding-top: calc(var(--sticky-h, 0px) + var(--space-2)) }`
  `html:has([data-surface-context]){ --sticky-h:56px }`
  `@media (max-width:767px){ html:has([data-surface-context]){ --sticky-h:88px } }`

`--sticky-h` defaults to `0px` so a surface with no sticky band — the reader —
gets 8px of air and nothing more. A surface that carries the shared
`data-surface-context` hook gets the static fallback. **The two numbers in this
contract are not in conflict and no fixture may assert either as the band
height:** the band measures roughly 38-40px at 768px and up, and the fallback is
56px/88px on purpose, because over-scrolling by 16px is invisible and
under-scrolling by 1px is the entire defect. Where `:has()` is unsupported
`--sticky-h` stays 0 and anchors land 8px clear rather than band-clear — worse
than the fix, never worse than today, and stated rather than hidden.

(2) **Scroll targets** get `scroll-margin-top` with the same calc, as the belt:

  in `quiz_page.py`'s style block: `.card`
  in `lesson.py`'s LESSON_CSS: `h2[id], h3[id], #glossary dt, .term[id], .gate`

Note the design contract writes that last one as `[id].term-use`; the reader's
first-use anchor is a `.term` element carrying `id="use-<slug>"`, so `.term[id]`
is the same element under the class the code actually uses.

(3) **The measured height**, in both `quiz_page.py` clients: one
`ResizeObserver` on `[data-surface-context]` writing
`document.documentElement.style.setProperty("--sticky-h", h + "px")`. Write only
when the rounded integer pixel height CHANGES, or an observer that triggers a
layout that re-triggers the observer becomes a loop. Guard the whole thing on
`"ResizeObserver" in window`.

(4) **Do not scroll a card that is already fully in view.** Guard BOTH
`scrollIntoView` call sites:

  read the card's bounding rect and the current `--sticky-h`; scroll only when
  the card's top is above the band or its bottom is past the viewport.

The first item of a sitting then produces no scroll at all, which is both
correct and calmer. Apply the identical guard in both clients — they are
mutually exclusive at runtime, and fixing one is how this class of defect
survives.
  </action>

  <files>surfaces/lesson.py, schemas/settings.schema.json, tests/lesson_roundtrip.py</files>

  <action>
`reader_nav: none | column` with default `none` was chosen in Phase 3.1 when
"there is no real bank yet … the length at which orientation starts paying is
unmeasured", and 03.1 said in terms "Revisit against the Phase 3.2 corpus."
Phase 3.2 has landed. This is the revisit, and per Directive §3 the two
defensible options are both shipped behind one setting rather than arbitrated.

(1) **Widen the enum** in `schemas/settings.schema.json` to
`none | column | rail | auto` with default **`auto`**, `x-itembank-phase: 13.5`,
and a description that states each value's rule. `none` and `column` are
inherited and behave exactly as they do today.

  `auto`   — render `column` when the lesson has **four or more `###` sections**,
             otherwise render nothing; render `rail` instead of `column` at
             1280px and wider. The count rule is a rule an executor implements in
             one line and a fixture asserts exactly; a word count is not.
  `rail`   — the same markup in the left margin outside the reading column,
             at 1280px and wider only; below that it renders `column`.

Why `rail` is now allowed, recorded so it is not re-litigated: 03.1 rejected it
for Phase 3.1 on two grounds — "the support column already owns that third" and
"a third responsive behaviour to test at three widths" — and recorded the
rejection as the reason a later shell revision might revisit it, calling it "the
most distinctive of the three". Both grounds are answered: the standalone reader
has no support column, and the rail is gated to a single breakpoint at which it
is the only variant, so it adds one responsive case rather than three.

(2) **`column`** (768-1279px, and 1280px and wider when `rail` is off). Placed
after `<h1>`/`.sub` and before the first section. Native `<details>`, **open by
default at 1024px and wider, closed below**. Summary in Ledger voice; list items
in **Chrome** voice, because the nav is the tool and not the author;
`list-style:none`; item rhythm `--space-2`; each anchor a real `<a href="#slug">`
with a **44px minimum row height** — this is a navigation control, so the inline
target exception does not apply. Render the section number in Ledger at the label
size beside the title.

(3) **`rail`** (1280px and wider). The same markup, `<details open>` with the
disclosure triangle suppressed, placed in a
`grid-template-columns: minmax(0,1fr) auto` shell so the reading column stays
exactly where `column` puts it and the rail occupies otherwise-empty margin.
`position:sticky; top:calc(var(--sticky-h, 56px) + var(--space-4));
max-height:calc(100vh - var(--sticky-h, 56px) - var(--space-6)); overflow:auto`.
Width `18ch`; titles wrap; no ellipsis. **The reading measure does not change
between `rail` and `column`** — that is what makes the rail free rather than a
second layout, and a fixture should assert the emitted `.wrap` max-width is
identical under both values.

(4) **Long lessons.** The nav scrolls inside itself in `rail`, and inside the
open `<details>` in `column` (`max-height:40vh; overflow:auto`), rather than
pushing the lesson off the first screen. Section titles wrap to as many lines as
they need — no `nowrap`, no ellipsis. Under Phase 6.2 gated reading the filtered
nav copy is inherited and unchanged.

(5) **Current-section highlighting is optional and, if present, must be
announced and not merely painted** (inherited): `aria-current="true"` on the
anchor plus a 2px `--accent` `border-inline-start`. **No scroll-spy** — a
`:target`-driven highlight is sufficient and needs no script. That is the
recorded default; do not add an observer.

(6) **Print stays `display:none`** — a printed page does not need a table of
contents of itself — and the rule stays INSIDE the `@media print` block. Scoping
it, not deleting it, is the fix for the leak that made this invisible.

(7) **In a sitting the ladder wins**, inherited and unchanged: when an item is
active the lesson is support, so the nav goes below the hint ladder, collapsed,
inside the in-flow support region, whose order is hints → document navigation →
evidence. This plan does not modify that ordering; plan 14-07 builds on it.

Fixtures: a three-section lesson under `auto` renders no nav; a four-section
lesson renders one; `rail` below 1280px renders the `column` markup; every nav
anchor is a real link with a 44px row; the emitted reading measure is identical
under `column` and `rail`; the print rule is inside the print block.
  </action>
