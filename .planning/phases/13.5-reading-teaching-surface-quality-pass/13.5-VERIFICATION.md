#### 2. Gloss placement and input modalities

**Test:** Exercise middle, right-edge, and bottom-edge terms at wide and narrow coarse-pointer viewports, using pointer, keyboard, and touch.
**Expected:** The gloss remains within 24px where anchored, flips inside the viewport, becomes a bottom sheet on narrow coarse pointers, and preserves the complete keyboard path.
**Why human:** Native anchor positioning, fallback selection, and coarse-pointer geometry require a real browser.

#### 3. Reader navigation layout

**Test:** Inspect four-section and nine-section lessons at wide, tablet, and narrow widths.
**Expected:** The rail occupies unused margin without shifting the reading measure, the narrow disclosure is reachable, and long navigation scrolls internally.
**Why human:** The layout relationship and usable geometry are not fully established by string fixtures.

#### 4. Script-free and no-network teaching loop

**Test:** On the loopback daemon, disable scripting and separately disable external network access. Submit a wrong practice answer, open two tiers, reach reveal, use a gloss, and return.
**Expected:** The authored loop remains complete, focus order and visible copy are correct, no keyed content leaks, and no unnecessary offline announcement appears.
**Why human:** The automated HTTP path proves server behavior, but not full browser focus, CSS-off text, or actual network isolation.

#### 5. Screen-reader announcement behavior

**Test:** With a real screen reader, exercise successful and failed runnable code, generated guidance, ordinary quiz feedback, and a blocking visual action failure.
**Expected:** Each relevant transition announces once, steady-state readouts remain quiet, and ordinary updates do not compete.
**Why human:** DOM mutation order is tested, but actual assistive-technology announcement behavior cannot be self-certified.
