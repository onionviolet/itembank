# Phase 16C: UI Design Contract

> Visual and interaction contract for frontend phases. Generated by
> gsd-ui-researcher, verified by gsd-ui-checker.

**Session mode:** autonomous (`--auto`). No `CONTEXT.md` exists for this
phase, matching the recorded precedent for Phases 14A through 16B. Every open
point below was resolved from `16C-RESEARCH.md` (recommendations and
assumptions binding unless a reasoned overturn is recorded), `16C-PATTERNS.md`,
`REQUIREMENTS.md` (GRAPH-03, NOTE-01..03, STRATEGY-01..02, UPGRADE-01..02),
`ROADMAP.md`'s Phase 16C section, `16B-UI-SPEC.md` (the binding upstream
contract: decisions D1-D9, route contract, mode-layer contract, conflict copy,
Activity naming rule, degraded-state matrix, copywriting contract),
`research/phase-16/08-curriculum-hierarchy-progress.md` sections 6.1-6.3,
`research/phase-16/12-active-annotation-notes.md` sections 7-11,
`STYLE-DISCIPLINE-16A-2026-08-14.md`, `DECISIONS-12.6-REMAINING-2026-08-14.md`
(D-12.6-5), and the project-level `UI-SPEC.md` sections 7 and 8, or decided as
a defensible default and recorded with its reasoning in the **Decisions &
Reasoning Log** near the end of this document, per `PLANNING-DIRECTIVES.md`
section 2 and the run's `--auto` instruction.

**Scope boundary (binding on this document).** Phase 16C is a **structural and
copy contract phase** for its learner-facing surfaces: the note capture and
review panels, the learner-artifact record view, the promotion review flow,
the strategy picker, the progress comprehension display, the note-output trio
render targets, and the legacy-upgrade review surface. It is explicitly
**not** a visual-token, palette, typography-scale, or component-styling
freeze; that is Phase 17A's job, exactly as `16B-UI-SPEC.md`'s scope boundary
and deferred table establish. Where this document would normally carry hex
values or pixel sizes, it carries the structural and copy contract those
tokens will later render, and points at the project-level `UI-SPEC.md` section
7 (voice/token system) as the fixed forward reference. Nothing below
introduces a new spacing, typography, or color token; section 7's four voice
tokens (`--font-paper`/`--font-ledger`/`--font-chrome`/`--font-code`) and five
semantic tokens (`--ok`/`--bad`/`--warn`/`--unknown`/`--pending`) are reused
and assigned to the new surfaces this phase introduces.

**Upstream contract, restated as hard constraints on every section below:**

- Nothing here contradicts 16B-UI-SPEC D1-D9. In particular: Notes get **no
  dedicated route** (16B D4); they render only as contextual panels inside
  Learn and Evidence. No percent-complete number appears anywhere (16B D7,
  GRAPH-03). Path-bearing copy shows the basename only, never a resolved
  absolute path (16B D9).
- The 16B conflict copy is reused verbatim and never re-worded: "{Setting
  name} is set by {higher layer name} for this course and can't be changed
  here."
- The 16B Activity naming rule carries forward: "Activity view" (capitalized)
  means the IA area for durable agent/maintenance jobs; "activity" (lowercase)
  means a learner task. This phase's per-action note states (`not_started`,
  `draft`, `completed`, `skipped_optional`, `equivalent_completed`,
  `needs_review`) are named **strategy-action states** in all copy, code, and
  fixtures (16C-RESEARCH Pitfall 6).
- The nine accessibility gates in project `UI-SPEC.md` section 8 are
  non-negotiable and bind every surface below.

## Note Capture Panel Contract (inside Learn)

Placement per 16B D4 (no dedicated Notes route) composed with D-12.6-5
recommendation C, adopted here as the reversible default (Decision D1):
**capture happens in place at the anchor, inside Learn; review happens in the
Notes panel inside Evidence.**

### Structure

1. **Capture affordance.** A single "Add a note" control is reachable from
   any lesson block: on pointer devices it appears with the block's focus or
   selection; on keyboard it is reachable in the normal tab order at the end
   of each block's controls; on touch it is a persistent per-block control
   with a 44px target. It is never hover-only (project `UI-SPEC.md` section 8
   narrow rule).
2. **Anchor selection, three equivalent paths (Decision D13).** The learner
   states what the note is about via any of: (a) pointer text selection, (b)
   a keyboard block-and-range picker ("Use arrow keys to choose a block, then
   Enter to select. Shift plus arrows extends the selection."), or (c) a
   structured choice list: "This selection" / "This block" / "This whole
   section" / "This objective (no anchor)". Drag-only highlighting is
   forbidden (report 12 section 11.1). All three paths produce the same
   multi-selector target record (stable ID, content fingerprint, locator,
   quoted-context hash per NOTE-01); the interaction path is never recorded
   as a difference in the note.
3. **Epistemic role choice (Decision D4).** A required single choice, with
   "My claim" preselected: "A quotation" / "My claim" / "My question" / "My
   example" / "A calculation" / "A diagram" / "A link to an accepted
   reference". These are the seven roles from report 12 section 7.2, in
   learner-facing words; the stored values are the verbatim role vocabulary
   (`quote`, `learner_claim`, `learner_question`, `learner_example`,
   `calculation`, `diagram`, `accepted_reference_link`).
4. **Privacy default (Decision D3).** Every note is private by default. The
   capture panel states this in one fixed line: "Private to you. Nothing is
   shared unless you request review." There is no privacy picker at capture
   time; scope changes happen per note from the Notes panel in Evidence.
5. **Save versus submit separation (Decision D2).** "Save to my notes" and
   "Submit activity" are always two separate controls and never merged. When
   an activity requires a submitted response, the submit control carries the
   clarifier: "Submitting shares this response with the course. Your saved
   notes stay private." (Report 12 section 11.2: separate "save to my notes"
   from "submit this activity.")
6. **Draft safety.** Note drafts autosave; an interruption never loses a
   draft (prototype A success gate). On return, the panel shows "Your draft
   note was restored." in Ledger voice.
7. **Authored pre-highlighting (Decision D14).** Author emphasis renders as
   sparse orientation with a concise screen-reader label per emphasized
   region (never an announcement on every styled word), produces zero
   evidence events and zero note ownership, and is never presented as
   something the learner did (clause C107).

### Accessibility (mandatory, gates 1-9 of project UI-SPEC section 8)

- Full keyboard path: block focus, "Add a note", anchor picker, role choice,
  text entry, save; no keyboard trap; focus returns to the anchored block
  after save.
- Screen readers receive the anchor's accessible description (block heading
  or term name), the chosen role, and the privacy line; they never receive
  any keyed content the block withholds.
- Touch path uses the structured block-choice list with 44px targets; no
  precision drag is ever required.
- Status updates ("Saved to your notes.") announce once via the single
  `role=status` region; never `role=alert` for a successful save.

## Notes Panel Contract (inside Evidence)

The review home for notes (Decision D1). No dedicated route (16B D4): this
panel renders inside `GET /course/<course_id>/evidence`.

### Structure

1. **Listing.** One row per note, grouped by objective, showing: the note's
   epistemic role label, its first line of learner wording (Paper voice),
   its anchor-state chip (table above), its privacy/promotion state badge,
   and its last-revised date. Rows wrap; long wording is truncated at the row
   level with the full note one activation away, never mid-word ellipsis in
   the role or state labels.
2. **Empty state.** "No notes yet for this course. Add one from any lesson in
   Learn."
3. **Broken-anchor flag states.** Each note carries exactly one of the four
   closed relocation states (NOTE-01, report 12 section 8.2), rendered per
   the Color table above. `relocated_probable` additionally renders two
   review affordances: "Confirm new location" and "Keep unanchored". A
   probable relocation is **never auto-applied** (Decision D6; Do-Not-Re-Open
   row 5, no automatic ambiguous merge). An `orphaned` note keeps its
   objective attachment visible in the same row: "Anchor lost. Still linked
   to {objective name}."
4. **Promotion request affordance.** Each private note row offers "Request
   review for course use". Requesting review changes the badge to "Review
   requested. Awaiting source-backed review." and nothing else: the note
   stays learner-private and non-authoritative until a reviewer accepts
   (NOTE-02 degraded contract).
5. **Deletion.** "Delete this note? This removes the note and its private
   index. Evidence the runtime is required to keep is not affected." Confirm
   button: "Delete note". (Report 12 section 11.2's deletion scope, stated
   honestly: lifecycle facts already in the append-only evidence store are
   not clawed back, and the copy says so rather than implying total erasure.)

## Promotion Review Flow Contract

The reviewer-side flow for NOTE-02. Reuses the reviewer-acceptance vocabulary
(Loop F: accept / reject / revise; journal outcomes applied / refused) and
the bounded-diff presentation shape, never a new review grammar (Decision D7).

1. **Entry.** From a note whose state is "Review requested", the reviewer
   opens "Review for course use".
2. **Source-backed check, shown before any accept control is enabled.**
   "Every keyed or factual claim needs an accepted source. {N} claims have no
   accepted source yet." While N > 0, "Accept into course" is disabled with
   this stated reason (never a silently disabled button, project `UI-SPEC.md`
   section 8 gate 9).
3. **Conflict stop.** If the note contradicts an accepted source: "A source
   conflict was found. Promotion is stopped until the conflict is resolved."
   (`--warn`). Promotion never proceeds by picking the note, the newest
   source, or a model output silently (report 12 section 7.2 item 5).
4. **Explicit accept.** Button "Accept into course" with confirmation:
   "Accept this note into the course? The original note stays yours; the
   course gets a cited copy with its derivation recorded." Acceptance is a
   distinct derived artifact with a derivation edge, never an in-place
   mutation of the learner's note.
5. **Decline.** Button "Decline" with a required reason field labeled
   "Reason (recorded with the decision)". Outcome badge on the note: "Not
   accepted: {reason}. Your note is unchanged." (`--bad`).
6. **Unreviewed default.** A note never enters this flow without the
   learner's request; an unreviewed note stays private and non-authoritative
   forever by default. No model settles promotion; a model may only annotate
   the review with labeled "Generated synthesis" material.

## Strategy Picker Contract

Renders at the entry to a Learn session (and wherever a lesson step offers a
strategy choice). The picker lists **only** what the composed precedence
resolver returns (STRATEGY-02); it never invents an option.

### Row classes (Decision D8)

| Row class | When | Rendering |
|---|---|---|
| Choosable | Strategy is registered, allowed by every layer, and available | Selectable row: strategy name (Chrome voice) plus its one-line purpose |
| Fallback | Strategy is allowed but unavailable (STRATEGY-01 degraded path) | Non-selectable row with "{Strategy name} isn't available right now. Continuing with continuous reading." (`--unknown`); continuous reading is preselected |
| Locked by a higher layer | A higher precedence layer disallows it | Read-only row carrying the 16B conflict copy verbatim: "{Setting name} is set by {higher layer name} for this course and can't be changed here." Example: "Retrieval first is set by your instructor's policy for this course and can't be changed here." |

### Fixed strings

- Heading: "How do you want to work through this?"
- Strategy names and one-line purposes (Chrome voice):
  - "Continuous reading": "Read straight through, with optional highlights
    and notes."
  - "Guided note spine": "Prompted selection and restatement as you go."
  - "Worked reasoning": "Predict, explain, and self-check worked steps."
  - "Retrieval first": "Try questions first, then read what you missed."
- Mid-sitting lock: "Strategy changes are paused during a test sitting."
  (STRATEGY-02: runtime assessment behavior is never user-configurable
  during a sitting; this line renders wherever the picker would otherwise
  appear during a formal sitting.)

### Rules

- The registry is the closed four-strategy set; the picker never renders a
  fifth option or a per-widget toggle matrix (report 12 section 10.4).
- One primary strategy plus at most one optional supplement per lesson step;
  the supplement, when offered, renders as a single labeled secondary choice,
  never a settings grid.
- Choosing a strategy is reversible at any safe transition (mode-layer
  table's learner-preference row); the picker restates the current choice on
  return.

## Progress Comprehension Display Contract (GRAPH-03)

Renders inside Evidence (course level) and on the course Overview's summary
region. Built from the independent claim tuple; the display is a pure
renderer over already-derived claims (16C-PATTERNS: `retention_view`
precedent, no arithmetic in the view).

### Structure

1. **One claim row per dimension, never merged.** The seven dimensions render
   as separate labeled rows: "Design coverage", "Participation", "Settled
   evidence", "Current retention", "Formal completion", "Selected
   enrichment", "Open uncertainty". No aggregate completion, mastery, or
   readiness number appears anywhere, including the region heading, the
   course card, and any tooltip (Decision D9; Do-Not-Re-Open row 1).
2. **Numerator and denominator in text, always.** Every determinate claim
   states its numbers in words: "{n} of {N} {metric name}", for example
   "9 of 12 required activities submitted". Color and any bar length are
   redundant with this text, never a substitute (report 08 section 6.3 item
   7). The metric name is specific, never the bare word "progress" (item 6).
3. **Separate denominators.** Required, required-choice, and enrichment
   memberships each keep their own denominator and their own row text;
   adding enrichment never changes a required denominator.
4. **Indeterminate state.** A claim with no denominator renders:
   "Indeterminate: {scope name} has no fixed denominator." (`--unknown`),
   never an invented percent and never an empty bar.
5. **Pending and unknown.** "{n} pending review" (`--pending`) and "Unknown"
   plus the stated reason (`--unknown`) render as their own counts, never
   folded into passes or failures. A version-split objective reads: "Unknown
   for this version of {objective name}. Earlier evidence stays with the
   earlier version."
6. **Per-objective fill state (D-14A-3).** Each objective renders the
   self-adjustable fill state as discrete filled blocks that move up and
   down as evidence and retention change. Its fixed legend: "Filled blocks
   show current standing for this objective. They move up and down as
   evidence and retention change. This is not a permanent grade." The block
   count and glyph rendering are 17A's; the contract fixes discrete blocks
   (never a continuous bar), self-adjustable in both directions, and the
   legend text.
7. **Retention and Retrievability.** Retention renders as a state
   distribution using the state words "due", "stable", "weak", "at risk",
   "unknown" with counts and the snapshot window. Retrievability is never
   surfaced as a percentage or probability number anywhere in the display.

### ARIA rules (Decision D10, from report 08 section 6.3)

- Each claim row is a named region whose accessible name is the metric name.
- A determinate claim uses `role=progressbar` with `aria-valuemin`,
  `aria-valuemax`, `aria-valuenow`, **and** `aria-valuetext` set to the full
  text "{n} of {N} {metric name}", because the bare number alone is not
  meaningful.
- The per-objective fill state uses `aria-valuetext` only (no
  `aria-valuenow`), stating the current standing in words, because the fill
  state is not a stable numeric claim.
- An indeterminate claim uses an indeterminate progressbar (no
  `aria-valuenow`) with visible explanatory text; never an unexplained
  spinner.
- State changes announce once through the single `role=status` region, not
  per animation frame.

## Note-Output Trio Render Target Contract

The three prototype projections (STYLE-DISCIPLINE, binding order): notebook
page, Cornell notes, concept map, each a validator plus a projection over the
one parsed content instance, zero re-parsing, zero per-style content forks.
This section fixes what each render target must show, its plain-Markdown
degradation, and its validator-failure presentation. Pixel layout is 17A's.

| Mode | Rich render must show | Plain-Markdown degradation | Validator failure fixture focus |
|---|---|---|---|
| Notebook page | Free sections in the learner's order; every block labeled with its epistemic role; every anchored block showing its anchor state chip and provenance (stable ID plus locator, Code voice, basename only); ownership always visible (whose words these are) | Headings, role labels as plain text prefixes, provenance as plain-text locator lines; fully coherent as a standalone Markdown document | ownership, anchors, provenance (an anchor to a moved block) |
| Cornell notes | Cue column, notes column, summary region; every cue visibly mapped to at least one note; cue-driven review order preserved | Linear degradation: cues render as headings, their notes as body text beneath, summary last; reads coherently top to bottom | every cue maps to notes (a cue without notes fails) |
| Concept map | Every node a named concept; every edge carrying its stated relation type as visible text, never an unlabeled line; keyboard traversal of nodes and edges; no drag-only construction | Textual adjacency fallback: each node as a heading followed by "relates to {node}: {relation type}" lines; coherent without any graphic | every edge typed (an unlabelled edge fails) |

**Validator-failure presentation (Decision D11).** When a mode's validator
fails, the rich view refuses and the surface renders: "The {mode name} view
can't be built from this content: {failing check, stated in the validator's
own words}. Showing plain Markdown instead." (`--warn`, Ledger voice),
followed by the plain-Markdown degradation of the same content. The failing
check is always stated ("a cue has no matching notes", "a relation has no
type", "an anchor points to a block that moved"), never a generic error.
This follows the shipped `RENDER_REFUSAL_COPY` precedent: a refusal names
what it cannot do and why, and the content remains fully readable in plain
form (derived views never become the only understandable copy,
Do-Not-Re-Open row 6).

**Accessibility.** Each rich render passes the same gates as its plain form:
the concept map's keyboard and screen-reader path is the textual adjacency
structure itself (no hover-only meaning, no drag-only construct,
Do-Not-Re-Open row 4); Cornell's cue-to-note mapping is expressed in
document structure, not visual columns alone.

## Legacy-Upgrade Review Surface Contract (UPGRADE-01/02)

Renders inside Build and review (`GET /course/<course_id>/build`), reusing
Loop F's draft-review shape and the DiffApproval contract from the project
`UI-SPEC.md` section 4; this phase adds the audit-first ordering and the
keyed-meaning halt (Decision D12).

1. **Baseline audit summary, always first.** Before any diff renders, the
   surface shows "Baseline audit" as a fixed-order checklist of the eleven
   items (current parse, identity, fingerprint, objectives, sources, rights,
   media, assessment boundaries, plain rendering, rich rendering,
   validation), each row carrying its recorded result in Ledger voice. Items
   whose backing modules are unexecuted (identity, rights before 14A/14B)
   read "plan-text stand-in" rather than an invented pass. Completion line:
   "All 11 baseline checks recorded."
2. **Bounded diff presentation.** "Proposed changes ({N})" renders the diff
   through the existing DiffApproval shape: each change shows before and
   after, its stated learning-value reason, and accept/reject controls.
   Never an unbounded rewrite; never a diff without its reason.
3. **Cosmetic rejection.** A change with no learning value renders as
   "Skipped: no learning value added." (`--unknown`) and is not proposed
   for acceptance (UPGRADE-01: cosmetic novelty rejected).
4. **Cannot-express state.** "This artifact can't express {enhancement} in
   its own form. It keeps its form; a derived enhancement is linked, with
   its portability cost stated." The original artifact is never mutated to
   force an enhancement it cannot carry.
5. **Halt-for-assessment-review state (UPGRADE-02).** If any proposed change
   would alter keyed content, difficulty, or objective alignment, the whole
   upgrade halts before acceptance: "Halted: this change would alter keyed
   assessment meaning ({what would change, stated}). Assessment changes are
   reviewed separately and are never part of an upgrade." (`--warn`, Ledger
   voice). The only affordances on the halted surface are "Open assessment
   review" and "Cancel upgrade". There is no override control; the halt is
   the contract, not a warning.
6. **Identity and history.** Every accepted upgrade preserves stable
   identity and source history, and the surface states the artifact's
   identity (Code voice, ID only) beside the diff so a reviewer can see it
   is unchanged.
