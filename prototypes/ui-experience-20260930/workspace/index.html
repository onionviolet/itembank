A faster walker covers more ground in every second. Keep the clock fixed and watch the gap grow.

YOUR AIM Explain how speed and elapsed time determine distance.
01 / THE RELATIONSHIP

Distance accumulates.

For constant speed, distance equals speed multiplied by elapsed time. Walker A moves at 3 metres per second. Walker B moves at 5 metres per second. After the same time, B has travelled farther because B adds more distance each second.

02 / PUT IT INTO WORDS

What stays the same?

Both walkers share the elapsed time. Their speeds stay fixed. The difference in their distances is therefore (5 − 3) × time.

Before you check.

At 8 seconds, how far ahead is Walker B?

The gap is 16 metres.

A travels 3 × 8 = 24 m. B travels 5 × 8 = 40 m. Subtract: 40 − 24 = 16 m.

A model of constant speed

When speed is constant, equal intervals of time correspond to equal distances travelled. The total distance is the product of speed and elapsed time.
