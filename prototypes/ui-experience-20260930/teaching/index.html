02 / CONNECT THE DOTS

Distance is speed × time.

Every second, Mira adds 3 metres and Noor adds 5. That extra 2 metres per second makes the gap grow.

Distance from the start · metres

At 6 seconds: Mira 18 m; Noor 30 m; gap 12 m.

Work it through

AT 6 SECONDS

3 m/s × 6 s = 18 m

Speed tells us the distance added in each second. Multiply by the number of seconds.

5 m/s × 6 s = 30 m

The time is the same for both walkers. Noor adds more distance every second.

30 m − 18 m = 12 m

Both started together, so subtract the distances to find the gap. Equivalently, (5 − 3) m/s × time.

Read the full explanation without the controls

Distance = speed × elapsed time. At 6 seconds, Mira has travelled 3 × 6 = 18 metres. Noor has travelled 5 × 6 = 30 metres. Their gap is 30 − 18 = 12 metres.

Noor travels 2 more metres each second, so gap = (5 − 3) × time = 2 × time. This holds because both start together, move in the same direction, and keep constant speed.

MODEL REFERENCE / R1

What does 3 m/s mean?

Three metres per second means adding 3 metres of distance in every second.

1 second3 metres2 seconds6 metres6 seconds18 metres

The rule

distance = speed × time

The seconds cancel: (metres / second) × seconds = metres.

The assumptions matter

Both walkers start at the same place and time, travel in the same direction, and never change speed. Different starts or changing speeds would need a different model.
