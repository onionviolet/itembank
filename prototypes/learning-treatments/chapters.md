## CH-WC-01: The water cycle is a moving system

**Objective `OBJ-WC-01`:** Trace water among atmosphere, land, and surface
storage using evaporation, condensation, precipitation, infiltration, and
runoff.

**Original synthetic source, locator `WFN-01 §1.2`:**

> A raindrop does not leave the water cycle when it reaches the ground. It
> changes route. Some water enters soil, some travels over the surface, and
> some returns to the air. The route depends on conditions at that place and
> time.

**Context:** This paragraph opens the source's first chapter before any local
rainfall comparison. It names routes, not a fixed order of events.

**Separate synthesis:** Sketch arrows only after naming the storage or place at
each end. An arrow without a destination can hide whether water infiltrated,
ran off, or evaporated.

**Synthetic prerequisite relationship:** None. This chapter starts the
specimen's water-route vocabulary.

## CH-RF-02: Read rainfall as an interval

**Objective `OBJ-RF-02`:** Compare rainfall observations only when their time
windows, units, and collection conditions are known.

**Original synthetic source, locator `WFN-01 §2.3`:**

> Rainfall totals are not self-explanatory. Before comparing two numbers, read
> the interval attached to each one. Twelve millimeters in one hour and twelve
> millimeters in one day describe different conditions.

**Context:** The following paragraph asks the reader to check units and whether
the gauge was read after a complete collection interval.

**Separate synthesis:** Put the interval beside every total before doing any
subtraction. The units can match while the question still does not.

**Synthetic prerequisite relationship:** None. This chapter is independently
foundational because interval reading does not require the water-route chapter.

## CH-WS-03: Follow water across a watershed

**Objective `OBJ-WS-03`:** Explain how slope, soil, vegetation, and paved
surfaces can change water's route after precipitation.

**Original synthetic source, locator `WFN-01 §3.1`:**

> A watershed boundary is a map of likely direction, not a promise that every
> drop will arrive at the same time. Soil, roots, pavement, and small channels
> alter the pace and route of water moving downhill.

**Context:** This paragraph introduces a source map before the chapter's two
synthetic hillside observations.

**Separate synthesis:** When comparing places, name one surface difference and
one path difference. That keeps a watershed explanation from becoming a list of
weather words.

**Synthetic prerequisite relationship:** `CH-WC-01`, because water-route and
storage terms are needed to explain how a watershed surface changes a path.

## CH-ST-04: Compare storm responses

**Objective `OBJ-ST-04`:** Use a shared rainfall interval and observed surface
conditions to make a bounded comparison of runoff response.

**Original synthetic source, locator `WFN-01 §4.2`:**

> A heavier response is not proof of one cause. Start with what was measured,
> keep the comparison window visible, and name the condition that could explain
> the difference.

**Context:** This is the caution immediately before a pair of synthetic runoff
observations. It limits the claim a learner can make from a small comparison.

**Separate synthesis:** Lead with the observation, then offer the condition as
a possible explanation. This preserves the difference between data and a cause.

**Synthetic prerequisite relationships:** `CH-RF-02`, because a shared
rainfall interval makes observations comparable, and `CH-WS-03`, because
watershed routes and surfaces supply candidate conditions for the difference.
